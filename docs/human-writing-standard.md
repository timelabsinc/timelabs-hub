# TimeLabs human-writing standard

## Why this exists

Every public or customer-facing TimeLabs draft should sound like a person who
knows the watch, the customer or the decision being discussed. It should not
sound like a model performing “good writing.” This standard applies to Hermes,
Reddit, Instagram, email, WhatsApp, product copy, blog articles, YouTube scripts
and founder posts.

The goal is better writing, not detector evasion. No word, punctuation mark or
sentence shape proves that text was generated. Pattern lists are review signals.
Specificity, point of view, evidence and fidelity to the source matter more than
swapping a few fashionable AI words.

## The human test

A TimeLabs draft is ready only when an editor can answer yes to these questions:

1. Can I tell who is speaking and why they care?
2. Does it contain details that could only come from this watch, customer,
   build, experiment or decision?
3. Is every factual claim supported by a source we actually have?
4. Does the structure suit the channel, instead of reading like a small essay?
5. Could we remove any sentence without losing meaning? If yes, remove it.
6. Does the ending create the right next action without summarising the draft?
7. Would Schezan or the assigned teammate comfortably say this out loud?

## Research summary

Research comparing human and model-written text finds recurring differences in
formality, lexical variety, repetition and stylistic variation. The exact
signals change with the model, prompt, language and genre, so a universal
checklist is not reliable. Rewriting also carries a factual risk: a model can
make prose feel smoother while changing the source meaning or adding details.

Editorial field guides add useful surface signals: exaggerated significance,
generic positive claims, vague attribution, promotional adjectives, neat
negative parallelisms, repeated three-part structures, over-sectioning,
formatting residue and canned introductions or conclusions. These signals are
most useful as prompts to inspect a passage, never as proof of authorship.

## Common AI words and phrase families

These are contextual review flags, not forbidden words. Keep one when it is the
plainest accurate choice. Rewrite it when it replaces a concrete fact.

### Inflated importance

- crucial, pivotal, vital, significant, profound, transformative
- cornerstone, testament, reminder, hallmark, beacon
- plays a key role, stands as, serves as
- underscores the importance, highlights the need, reflects a broader shift

Human alternative: state what changed, for whom, and by how much. “The crown no
longer catches on a shirt cuff” carries more information than “a significant
improvement in wearability.”

### Generic polish and promotion

- elevate, unlock, empower, leverage, revolutionise
- seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changing, innovative
- meticulously crafted, thoughtfully curated, stunning, breathtaking
- boasts, renowned, vibrant, diverse array, rich heritage
- perfect blend, seamless fusion, where X meets Y

Human alternative: name the component, finish, constraint or result. Replace
“meticulously crafted for timeless elegance” with what was actually done, such
as “we brushed the top surface and kept the polished bevel narrow.”

### Abstract landscape language

- in today’s world, in the modern era, in an ever-evolving landscape
- in the realm of, when it comes to, at its core
- navigate the complexities, delve into, dive deep
- sheds light on, paves the way, marks a new chapter

Human alternative: start at the event. “We rebuilt this one after the first dial
arrived scratched” gives the reader a reason to continue.

### Mechanical transitions and throat-clearing

- moreover, furthermore, additionally, consequently
- it is worth noting, it is important to remember
- that said, with that in mind, at the end of the day
- as we have seen, as mentioned earlier

Human alternative: delete the transition when the order is already clear. If a
relationship needs to be stated, use the relationship: “Because the case is
thin,” “But the first version failed,” or “The customer chose blue.”

### Model-to-user residue

- certainly, here is a polished version, below is
- I hope this helps, let me know if you would like
- as an AI, I cannot, based on the information provided
- insert detail here, your company name, placeholder copy

These should never reach a finished draft.

## AI sentence and paragraph patterns

Review a draft for combinations of these habits:

- **Negative parallelism:** “not just X, but Y,” “it is not X; it is Y,” or
  “more than just X.” It manufactures significance without proving it.
- **The automatic three:** three adjectives, three questions, three examples or
  three clauses in repeated cadence. Use the number the material actually needs.
- **Restatement:** sentence two paraphrases sentence one instead of advancing it.
- **Detached analysis:** a fact is followed by an abstract “highlighting” or
  “underscoring” clause that says why it is supposedly important.
- **Uniform rhythm:** every sentence is medium length and every paragraph has
  the same shape. Human speech speeds up, stops, qualifies and changes direction.
- **Elegant variation:** the same thing is renamed on every mention to avoid
  repetition, making the writing less precise. Call an NH35 an NH35.
- **Vague authority:** “experts say,” “research shows” or “many believe” without
  naming a source that the editor can open.
- **Claim laddering:** an observable detail becomes an unsupported quality,
  performance or market claim in the next sentence.
- **Essay scaffolding:** introduction, three balanced sections and a conclusion
  when a direct answer, caption or short story would work better.
- **Reader simulation:** fake intimacy such as “we have all been there” or
  “you know that feeling” without a real shared situation.

## Formatting habits

- Too many headings for a short piece.
- Every paragraph converted into a bullet list.
- Bold fragments used as inline section labels.
- Title Case On Nearly Every Heading.
- Stacked emoji, decorative separators or repeated rhetorical questions.
- Markdown or assistant residue in a channel that does not render it.
- Identical paragraph lengths and perfectly balanced sections.
- Em dashes used repeatedly as a default sentence joint.

Formatting is channel-specific. A product specification can be a list. A Reddit
showcase should not look like a product specification pasted into a post.

## Channel habits and better alternatives

### Email and WhatsApp

Weak habits: “I hope this email finds you well,” repeating the subject in the
first line, a long context block before the request, artificial warmth, three
closing summaries and “please do not hesitate to reach out.”

Use the person’s name when appropriate, state the reason early, give only the
context needed for the decision, make one clear request and close naturally.
Customer support must acknowledge the actual problem before explaining policy.

### LinkedIn and founder posts

Weak habits: a manufactured confession, one-sentence paragraphs throughout,
stacked hooks, “I learned three powerful lessons,” false vulnerability,
engagement bait and a moral that could fit any founder.

Start from a real decision, mistake, customer moment or number. Keep the awkward
detail that makes the story recognisable. The lesson can stay implicit.

### YouTube scripts

Weak habits: “In today’s video,” an inflated promise, repeated previews of what
is coming, listicle chapters with identical transitions, scripted excitement and
a recap that repeats the whole video.

Open on the object, conflict or result. Write for breath and footage, not a blog
read aloud. Mark what should be shown. Let the presenter react to specific
details and end after the answer or next experiment.

### Blog and search content

Weak habits: defining a familiar term before answering, keyword-shaped headings,
generic history, repeated summaries, empty “challenges and future outlook”
sections and citations that do not support the sentence.

Answer the search question early. Use first-party build knowledge, measurements,
photos, tables or failure cases where they help. Every sourced claim needs an
openable source. Delete sections that exist only to make the article longer.

### Instagram captions and stories

Weak habits: a luxury-adjective pile, explaining everything visible in the
photo, a row of generic emojis, hashtag stuffing and the same “DM us” ending on
every post.

Choose one job: reveal a build choice, show a before/after, explain a component,
answer a buyer objection, tell a customer story or ask a precise preference.
The visual carries part of the meaning; the caption should add what the image
cannot show. Stories should use shorter beats and one interaction at a time.

### Reddit and community replies

Weak habits: marketing voice, a feature list, excessive background, brand links,
generic praise, “what do you think?” and pretending certainty about unseen
parts.

Reddit rewards useful specificity and honest participation. Name the part or
problem, say what was tried, disclose the brand relationship when relevant and
ask the question a watch builder would genuinely answer. A short reply that
solves someone’s problem is more valuable than a polished brand speech.

### Product copy

Weak habits: luxury filler, unsupported superlatives, describing a custom mod as
a factory Seiko watch, invented scarcity and a paragraph that could describe
every SKU.

Lead with the distinguishing build detail. State movement, case, dial, size,
finish, compatibility and delivery facts accurately. Explain how a detail feels
or wears only when observation or customer evidence supports it. Keep the
custom-construction and non-affiliation disclosure plain.

## Weak introductions and human alternatives

| Weak opening | Better route |
| --- | --- |
| “In today’s fast-paced world…” | Start with the event, object or decision. |
| “Have you ever wondered…?” | Ask only if a real answer is expected. Otherwise answer it. |
| “Introducing our latest masterpiece.” | Name what is new and why it was built. |
| “When it comes to watches…” | Begin with this watch. |
| “We are thrilled to announce…” | State the announcement and the useful detail. |
| “Picture this…” | Show the actual scene, measurement, photo or mistake. |

Example: “The first blue dial arrived scratched. We kept the case and rebuilt
the watch around this skeleton dial instead.”

## Weak conclusions and human alternatives

Avoid “in conclusion,” a tidy recap, “the future looks bright,” generic advice,
forced inspiration and “let us know what you think.” End with the natural next
beat:

- Reddit showcase: one specific build question.
- Founder note: the decision now being made, or the unresolved tension.
- Email: the requested action and date.
- Product page: specifications, delivery information or a relevant collection.
- Blog: the answer, a tested next step or a genuinely related guide.
- YouTube: the result, the next build or the action shown on screen.

## Natural sentence rhythm

- Mix short statements with longer explanatory sentences because the thought
  needs it, not to satisfy a formula.
- Put the important noun and verb early.
- Use contractions where the speaker would use them.
- Repeat a precise noun when a synonym would blur the meaning.
- Allow a small qualification, correction or aside when it reflects the truth.
- Prefer ordinary verbs: is, has, made, tried, failed, changed, chose.
- Read it aloud once. If the speaker needs a second breath or would never use
  the phrase in conversation, rewrite it.

## Conversational examples

Generic: “This meticulously crafted timepiece seamlessly blends timeless
elegance with modern innovation.”

TimeLabs: “The dial is the reason we built this one. Its open centre exposes
more of the NH movement, so we kept the case and hands quiet.”

Generic: “We are thrilled to unveil a game-changing addition to our collection.”

TimeLabs: “This is our first 36 mm build with the fluted bezel. We made it after
several customers asked for the same look on a smaller wrist.”

Generic Reddit ending: “What do you think of this stunning build? Let us know in
the comments!”

TimeLabs Reddit ending: “I am still split on the silver hands. Would you keep
them, or use black hands so the dial gets all the attention?”

## Facts and citation safeguards

Use this evidence order:

1. Current TimeLabs build record, measurement or approved product source.
2. Something clearly visible in the attached media.
3. Permissioned customer evidence.
4. A named, openable primary or credible secondary source.
5. A clearly labelled inference.

Never infer movement, material, water resistance, durability, price, origin or
compatibility from a photo. Never invent a quote, metric, customer reaction,
competitor fact or citation. If a required detail is missing, ask once. If it is
optional, omit it. After any style rewrite, compare every claim with the source
material because smoother copy is not necessarily faithful copy.

## Required workflow

### Before drafting

1. Select the channel and speaker: brand, founder, teammate or customer support.
2. Select one route: showcase, honest review, build diary, comparison,
   educational answer, founder note, customer story, launch or precise question.
3. State the audience and the one job the piece must do.
4. Collect the facts, media observations, source links and prohibited claims.
5. Choose the one detail or tension that makes this piece specific.

### Drafting

1. Draft from the evidence and route, not from a generic request to “write a
   post.”
2. Match the native shape of the channel.
3. Keep claims within the proof hierarchy.
4. Do not ask the model to imitate an individual or conceal AI use. Ask it to
   preserve the named speaker’s approved voice and facts.

### Editing and approval

1. Run the machine-checkable review. Treat matches as editor prompts, not an
   authorship verdict.
2. Remove assistant residue, placeholders, unsupported claims and vague
   attribution. These are blocking.
3. Inspect phrase clusters, rhythm, repetition, structure and the ending.
4. Compare the edited draft with the facts after the final rewrite.
5. Read it aloud and make a human edit.
6. Require a person to approve public publishing. Founder voice and material
   brand claims require owner approval.

## Machine-checkable taxonomy

The canonical implementation lives in `writing_quality.py`.

| Code family | What it flags | Default treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `residue.*` | Assistant narration, placeholders or AI self-reference | Block |
| `claim.vague_source` | Unnamed research, experts or consensus | Block until sourced/rephrased |
| `intro.generic` | Canned scene-setting or inflated announcement | Review |
| `outro.generic` | Recap, generic inspiration or empty engagement ask | Review |
| `phrase.inflated` | Importance, landscape, promo or transition phrase | Review; block in Reddit drafts |
| `structure.parallelism` | “Not X but Y” significance pattern | Review; block in Reddit drafts |
| `structure.rule_of_three` | Repeated triples or question stacks | Review |
| `structure.uniform_rhythm` | Unnaturally similar sentence lengths | Review |
| `format.excess` | Excess headings, bold labels, emoji or separators | Review |
| `evidence.unsupported` | Claim outside the provided proof set | Human/model factual audit; not regex-only |
| `fidelity.changed` | Rewrite no longer matches source facts | Source comparison; block |

## Labs OS coverage

The standard is enforced at the shared generation paths, not left to each
operator to remember:

- **Command:** every admin and team prompt receives the base standard. Slash
  routes and recognizable free-form writing requests select channel guidance
  for Instagram captions/stories, Meta ads, Reddit, WhatsApp, one-to-one sales,
  email, blog, YouTube, LinkedIn/founder and product copy. If the first draft
  matches an editorial flag, Command runs one fact-preserving edit pass.
- **Reddit:** post, reply and subreddit-setup pipelines receive the Reddit
  standard before drafting and retain the stricter deterministic final gate.
- **Product Builder:** AI product-photo listings receive the product standard
  before structured JSON is generated. Shopify creation and publishing remain
  separate admin-only actions.
- **Hermes CMO:** the canonical rule is stored in preference memory and this
  document is embedded in Command context.

Drop photo indexing, the Instagram audit, Blog uploader and Content updater are
not copy generators: they produce search metadata, show read-only analysis or
save text a person supplied. Their output should not be described as
“humanized.” If a new tool starts generating customer-facing copy, it must call
`writing_quality.prompt_brief(channel)` and run the editorial review before it
is registered as complete.

## Sources and limits

- [Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) is a practical field guide. It explicitly warns that its signs are descriptive and not proof.
- [AI-generated content on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI-generated_content_on_Wikipedia) documents recurring quality problems including fabricated citations, promotional language and formatting/style residue.
- [Understanding the Linguistic Fingerprints of Text Generated by Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05136) surveys linguistic differences and important limits in current research.
- [ChatGPT: Jack of all trades, master of none](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07799) finds greater stylistic variation in human text and warns that style rewriting can introduce factual errors.
- [Grammarly: Common AI words](https://www.grammarly.com/blog/ai/common-ai-words/) is a practical editorial list, not a scientific detector; it is useful for phrase-level review only.
- [Artificial intelligence content detection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_content_detection) summarises why detector output is not a reliable authorship verdict.

Most published comparison research is English-heavy, model-specific and
prompt-sensitive. TimeLabs should update this document when its own approved and
rejected drafts provide stronger first-party evidence.
